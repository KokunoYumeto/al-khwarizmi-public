It is fitting that the man of understanding should give his attention to what keeps the duties of his religion in good order and by which he keeps alive the ways of the righteous among those who went before him. When he does so, God takes it upon Himself to give him what suffices him, supports him with provision and grants him the reward of both abodes, this world and the hereafter.
@p P003-L05..P003-L15 + P004-L01..P004-L16
God, blessed and exalted is He, said in the Torah, in the first book: «Let there be light in the firmament, as a division between the night and the day, and as a sign of the times, the days and the years.»[^n] Then God Almighty commanded Moses, peace be upon him, in the fifth book, which confirms the books before it, to keep the month of the flowers, which is the month of Nisan, in which the month is renewed, the trees put forth their leaves, the earth splits open with its blossoms and the barley ripens; and to keep in the fifteenth night of it a Passover to his Lord, for the favour that God bestowed on him and on the children of Israel in bringing them out of the land of Egypt by night; and that this should fall at the filling of the moon and the completion of its light. He made it the head of the months and sent down the revelation of it in the first book. Then He commanded in the second book that this night be kept for ever, with many verses of the Torah in which He confirmed it, for He wished to choose the children of Israel, to try them and to test their obedience in the way He set for them, so as to reward them for what they do. So the prophet of God, peace be upon him, could not do without using the year of the sun and the year of the moon and making their reckoning clear, «{ar:والصاحة}»,[^n] and for the changes of the years, in which the explanation of the procedure will follow, so that the Passover should be in the month of the flowers, on the night of the fifteenth of Nisan, with fourteen nights of the lunar month [gone]. This differs from the reckoning of the Greeks and the Persians, because they keep to the year of the sun and its months alone, whether these agree with the months of the new moons or differ from them. So he, God bless him, commanded that a reckoning be set up to show the course of the sun and the moon, the number of the days of each of them, in how many days, hours and parts of these they come together when they have parted, the positions of the seven planets, and the beginning of the years from the day on which Adam was created. In every nineteen lunar years he put an addition of seven months, and he called the nineteen [years] with their addition the small *maḥzūr*, which means «the cycle». The year in which there is the addition of a month[^n] of the seven months is called the intercalated year, and he called that added month the last Adar, because the congregation of the children of Israel need to know it, and because it shows their days and their festivals, the entrances of the beginnings of their months and the years of their era, as century followed century.[^n]
@n The words are Genesis 1:14. The print has «{ar:لكن الصبا فى ربيع}», «but the east wind in spring», which gives no sense here; the translation reads {ar:لتكن}, «let there be», {ar:رقيع}, «the firmament» (Hebrew *raqiaʿ*, which Arabic versions of the Bible keep), and, with less certainty, {ar:الضياء}, «the light», which differs from {ar:الصبا} only in its dots.
@n The sense of «{ar:والصاحة}» is not clear ({ar:والصحة}, «and the soundness»? {ar:المصالحة}, «the reconciliation»?); it is left untranslated. In the next words {ar:ولغير السنين} is read as «and for the changes of the years».
@n The print has {ar:اشهر}, «months»; the next words name «that added month».
@n The print has {ar:فضت}; the translation reads {ar:مضت}, «passed», which differs from it by one dot.

The first of this is the naming of the months of the children of Israel and the number of the days of each month.[^n] The first of them is Nisan, which has 30 days; Iyyar, 29 days; Sivan, 30 days; Tammuz, 29 days; Av, 30 days; Elul, 29 days; Tishri, 30 days. If the year is regular, a full month and a deficient month in turn, Marheshvan[^n] has 29 days, Kislev 30 days, Tevet 29 days, Shevat 30 days and Adar 29 days. If the year exceeds the regular one by a day, Marheshvan has 30 days and Kislev 30 days.

As for the year of the moon: when it has twelve months, it is three hundred and fifty-four days, eight hours and 876 parts; when it has thirteen months, its days are 383 days, 21 hours and 589[^n] parts. As for the small *maḥzūr*, it is nineteen years with their intercalations, which make in years of the moon nineteen years and seven months, and the number of its days is 6939 days, sixteen hours and 595[^n] parts.

Every Tishri of a year that has an intercalation: if its moon is born before 491 [parts] have passed of the ninth hour of the day of Friday, the first of Tishri is on Saturday, and Marheshvan and Kislev are deficient. If there is no intercalation in that year nor in the following year, and the moon is born before 408 parts have passed of the first hour of the night of Friday, the first of Tishri is on Saturday and Marheshvan and Kislev are deficient; and if the moon is born after 408[^n] up to the limit of the day of Saturday, the first of Tishri is on Saturday and Marheshvan and Kislev are full. If there is no intercalation in the year and there is an intercalation in the following year, and the moon is born before 204 [parts have passed of the tenth hour of the night of Friday, the first of Tishri is on Saturday and Marheshvan and Kislev are deficient; and if the moon is born after 204][^n] up to the limit of the day of Saturday, Marheshvan and Kislev are full.

Every Tishri of a year that has an intercalation: if its moon is born before 960 parts have passed of the eleventh[^n] hour[^n] of the night of Wednesday, the first of Tishri 11200000000[^n] is on Thursday and Marheshvan and Kislev are deficient; and if the moon is born after [ ][^n] have passed of the eleventh hour of the night of Wednesday, up to the limit of the day of Thursday, the first of Tishri is on Thursday and Marheshvan and Kislev are full. Every Tishri of a year that has an intercalation: if its moon is born before the seventh hour of the day of Tuesday, the first of Tishri is on Tuesday and Marheshvan and Kislev are as in the regular year. If there is no intercalation in that year, and its moon is born before 204 [parts] have passed of the tenth hour of the night of Tuesday, the first of Tishri is on Tuesday and Marheshvan and Kislev are as in the regular year; and if the moon is born after 204 parts have passed of the [tenth][^n] hour of the night of Tuesday, the first of Tishri is on Thursday and Marheshvan and Kislev are as in the regular year.

Every Tishri of a year that has an intercalation: if its moon is born before 491 parts have passed of the ninth hour of the day of Sunday, the first of Tishri is on Monday and Marheshvan and Kislev are deficient; and if the moon is born after 491 parts have passed of the ninth hour of the day of Sunday, up to the limit of the day of Monday, the first of Tishri is on Monday and Marheshvan and Kislev are full. If there is no intercalation in that year and its moon is born before 204 parts have passed of the tenth hour of the night of Sunday, the first of Tishri is on Monday and Marheshvan and Kislev are deficient; and if its moon is born after 204 parts have passed of the tenth hour of the night[^n] of Sunday, up to the limit of the day of Monday, the first of Tishri is on Monday and Marheshvan and Kislev are full. If there is no intercalation in that year, and there was an intercalation in the year that went before it, and the birth of the moon is after 589[^n] parts have passed of the fourth hour of the day of Monday, the first of Tishri is on Tuesday and Marheshvan and Kislev are as in the regular year.
@n The print has 489 ({ar:٤٨٩}). Thirteen months of 29 days 12 hours 793 parts make 383 days 21 hours 589 parts (13 × 793 = 10,309 parts = 9 hours 589 parts), as does the twelve-month year plus one month (876 + 793 = 1669 parts = 1 hour 589 parts). They follow the parallel sentence above and are not attested. @n The print has 89 ({ar:٨٩}). As for the year of the sun, the number of its days is 365 days, 5 hours and 3791 parts of 4104 [in an] hour.[^n] The years that have passed from the time when God created Adam until the year one thousand one hundred and thirty-five of Dhū l-Qarnayn ends are 4082[^n] years with their intercalations, according to what is in the Torah, the books of the prophets and «the reports of now».[^n] The mean sun on the first of the days of Adam, which is Friday, was 5^s 26°;[^n] the mean moon 5^s 26°; the apogee of the moon 1^s 5°; Saturn 8^s 55;[^n] Jupiter 6^s 5°; Mars 1^s 6°; Venus 4^s 25°; Mercury[^1]; the head 5^s 14°. The mean sun at the building of the Temple, 5^s 26°; the moon [5^s][^n] 26°; the apogee of the moon 9^s 26° 40′ 16″; Saturn 10^s 22° 9′; Jupiter {ar:ج ـ رمت لد};[^n] Mars {ar:يخ انه كور}; Venus {ar:رنب يامر}; Mercury {ar:الج يط اط}; the head 4^s 26° 34′ 51″. The mean sun at the first of the years of Dhū l-Qarnayn, 6^s 18° 31′ 38″; the moon 4^s 6° 45′ 49″; the apogee of the moon {ar:ر كو ير يط};[^n] Saturn 8^s 8° 24′ 6″; Jupiter 3^s 12° 52′ 38″ 33‴; Mars 8^s 12° 14′ 46″; Venus 2^s 1° 22′ 3″; Mercury {ar:ر ى الح}; the head 4^s 23° 41′ 27″.
@n That is, 365 days 5 hours and 3791/4104 of an hour, about 365 days 5 hours 55 minutes 25 seconds. It is the year of the base of the days given below: 35,975,351 days in 98,496 revolutions of the sun leave, after 365 days in each, 24,311 days, which are 5 hours and 3791/4104 of an hour in each year (98,496 = 24 × 4104).
@n The figure stands as printed. The Jewish reckoning puts 3448 years between the creation of Adam and the era of Dhū l-Qarnayn (so Elias of Nisibis, reported by de Blois), which gives 4583 years at the end of the year 1135; the treatise's own rule below, which adds nine to the completed years of Dhū l-Qarnayn before casting out nineteens, agrees with 3448 years (3448 = 19 × 181 + 9), while 4082 − 1135 = 2947 leaves 2. The author's own figure is not known.
@n «{ar:واخبار الآن}» gives no clear sense; whether a word is missing or «now» begins the next sentence is not clear. With one dot less, {ar:واحبار الآن} would be «and the rabbis of the present».
@n The positions are written in the letters of the abjad: signs (^s, of 30 degrees), degrees, minutes, seconds and thirds. 5^s 26° is 5 signs 26 degrees, that is, 176° from the beginning of Aries.
@n Thus the print, {ar:ح نه}: 55 cannot be a number of degrees, and whether the degrees are missing or a letter is misread is not known.
@n The print has no sign here; at the building of the Temple, as on the first day of Adam, the sun and the moon are in conjunction at 5 signs 26 degrees.

@p P009-L07..P009-L18
Whoever wishes to know the mean position of the sun and the mean of the moon, let him take the completed years of Dhū l-Qarnayn and always add nine to them; then let him cast out nineteen years at a time from the sum. What remains, less than nineteen years, are lunar years of the working of the *maḥzūr*: let him turn them into lunar days, and the result is the small base. Multiply it by the revolutions of whichever of the two you wish to know the mean of, and divide the product by the base of the days: what comes out is solar years; cast them out. Then multiply what remains by twelve and divide it by the base of the days: what comes out is signs. Multiply what remains by thirty and divide it by the base: what comes out is degrees. Multiply what remains by sixty and divide it by the base: what comes out is minutes. Then we find in the same way as many seconds, thirds and fourths as you like. What comes out of signs, degrees and minutes, add to the position of whichever of the two you have referred the era to:[^n] the sum is its mean [position] at sunrise, if God wills.
@n That is, to its position at the beginning of the years of Dhū l-Qarnayn, given above.

The revolutions of the moon are the months of the base together with the revolutions of the sun: 1,218,240 + 98,496 = 1,316,736; and 35,975,351 / 1,316,736 = 27.3216 days, the period in which the moon goes round the zodiac.

If you wish to know the conjunction of the sun and the moon, which is the beginning of the month of the children of Israel, multiply the small base by twenty-five thousand nine hundred and twenty, and divide the product by seven hundred and sixty-five thousand[^n] four hundred and thirty-three: what comes out is the months that have passed from the beginning of the *maḥzūr* to the month you are in. Divide what remains by twenty-five thousand nine hundred and twenty: what comes out is days. Divide what remains by one thousand and eighty: what comes out is hours. What comes out of days, hours and parts of the hour is what has passed of your month since the conjunction, if God wills.
@n The print has «days» ({ar:يوما}) for «thousand» ({ar:الفا}). The divisor is the number of parts in a lunar month: 29 × 25,920 + 12 × 1080 + 793 = 765,433 (25,920 parts in a day).

For a better understanding of these questions I give below a short account of the motion of an upper planet according to Ptol. and Batt.; I follow Nallino as closely as possible.[^47.1]
@p 0079L12..0079L39 + 0080L01..0080L05
It is assumed that the planet moves on an *epicycle* whose centre[^47.2] *H* moves with unequal angular velocity, both with respect to the point *M* and with respect to the point *E*, on the circle with the centre *M* that is eccentric with respect to the earth *E*, the so-called *deferent*. But the centre *H* moves uniformly on a third, fictitious circle (I leave it out of the drawing) with the centre *E′* (*E′M* = *ME*), the so-called *equant*; or better, the motion of the point *H* appears uniform as seen from this point *E′*. Both the planet on the epicycle and the centre of the epicycle on the deferent move from west to east. The two uppermost planets (Jupiter and Saturn) complete their circuit on the epicycle in about one year, Mars, on the other hand, in c. 2 years (780 days); the centres of their epicycles complete their circuit on the deferent in different times, which agree fairly well with the present sidereal periods.[^n] *EM* is the eccentricity of the orbit of the planet, *EE′* twice that. The *true apogee* of the epicycle is, for every position of the point *H*, the point at which *EH* produced meets the ecliptic (in our figure, then, *R*)[^n], the *mean apogee* the point at which *E′H* produced meets the ecliptic (in our figure, then, *Q*). The two points coincide when the centre of the epicycle is at the apogee *G* or at the perigee *K* of the deferent. Let *AFB* be the ecliptic, and let the apogee *G* have the longitude *FA* in the ecliptic; the centre *H* of the epicycle has, by its mean motion about the centre *E′* of the equant, the longitude *FQ*, but by its true motion, as seen from the centre of the earth *E*, the longitude *FR*; the difference between the two, the mean and the true longitude, that is, the arc *RQ*, is called by Batt. the *equation of the anomaly and of the centre*, or just the *equation of the centre*; it is also the difference between the *mean* and the *true apogee* of the epicycle. — This equation is measured by ∢ *E′HE*; if one draws *MS* and *EN* perpendicular to *QHE′* produced, one has from the right-angled triangle *ENH*:

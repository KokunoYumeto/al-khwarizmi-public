On the conversion of the years of birth
@c 0134L22
(Table 115.)
@p 0134L23..0134L33
Table 115 teaches how to determine the place in the heavens that the sun occupies after a number of full years have elapsed, for example on the 35th birthday of a person, or, in other words, how many degrees (or how much time) it still has to run until it has arrived again at the same point (fixed star) at which it stood at the birth. Understood from the purely astronomical standpoint, one can also regard this table as a table for converting civil (Julian) years into sidereal years. Batt. has (II, 188) a similar table for converting civil years into tropical years, but its *anni collecti* proceed from 20 to 20 instead of from 10 to 10 as in our table, and the excesses over the full civil years are given only in degrees of arc, whereas in our table they are also given in measure of time.
@p 0134L34..0134L37
*Example:* Someone was born on 4 January 962 at a certain hour; how much time must he still wait, from the same hour of 4 Jan. 997, until the sun has arrived at the same place in the heavens, that is, at the same fixed star, at which it stood at the moment of the birth?
@p 0134L38..0134L39 + 0135L01..0135L08
Table 115 gives for 30 years (in the column *anni deceni*, the years by tens) 7$^{d}$ 18$^{h}$ 4$^{m}$, for 5 years (in the column *anni singulares*, the single years) 1$^{d}$ 7$^{h}$ 1$^{m}$, together 9$^{d}$ 1$^{h}$ 5$^{m}$. Only 9$^{d}$ 1$^{h}$ 5$^{m}$ after his 35th birthday[^n], then, does the sun stand at the same fixed star as at his birth. This time, however, undergoes a further correction through the determination of the *true* place of the sun, for the place found is only the *mean* one; but I shall not go into this further, and refer for it to Batt. I, 148. The second half of the table gives the path still to be run by the sun in degrees, minutes and seconds. — These *revolutiones* or *conversiones annorum nativitatum* (revolutions or conversions of the years of the nativities), and likewise the *revolutiones annorum mundi* (or *civitatum*) (revolutions of the years of the world, or of the cities), played a great part in the astrology of antiquity and of the Middle Ages[^103.1].
@n Table 115 counts the excess over years of 365 days: with al-Khwārizmī's year of 365.2584375 days (p. 42), 30 years exceed 30 × 365 days by 7$^{d}$ 18$^{h}$ 4$^{m}$, and 5 years exceed 5 × 365 days by 1$^{d}$ 7$^{h}$ 1$^{m}$. From 4 January 962 to 4 January 997 the Julian calendar counts 35 × 365 days and 9 leap days; the 9$^{d}$ 1$^{h}$ 5$^{m}$ thus run from 26 December 996, the end of 35 years of 365 days, and the sun returns to its place of the birth 1$^{h}$ 5$^{m}$ after the same hour of 4 January 997. The «civil (Julian) years» of p. 102 are likewise years of 365 days.

It is still to be mentioned here that in this table the excess of the sidereal year over the civil year is taken as 6$^{h}$ 12$^{m}$, or, if we convert the 93° 2′ 15″ of the last column into time, more exactly as 6$^{h}$ 12$^{m}$ 9$^{s}$, whereas by chapters 20 and 21 6$^{h}$ 13$^{m}$ 2$^{s}$ would have been expected. @c 0135L17
(Table 116.)
@p 0135L18..0135L20
This table is on the whole identical with the *astrological rose* in Batt., as I have already mentioned above; but in single points, which are still to be named, the two differ from each other.
@p 0135L21..0135L30
The 1st column[^103.3] contains the twelve signs of the zodiac, which in the rose named stand outside the outermost circle, which is accordingly divided into twelve sectors. In the 2nd column stand the lords (*domini*) of the corresponding houses (*domicilia*) of the 1st column; it would therefore rather have to be headed *domini* instead of *domicilia*; thus the Ram (*Aries*) is the house of *Mars*, or *Mars* is the lord of the house of the *Ram*, *Venus* that of the *Bull* (*Taurus*), and so on. The sun and the moon have only *one* house each, but the five planets *two* each; thus besides the *Ram* the *Scorpion* too is the house of *Mars*, and besides the *Bull* the *Balance* (*Libra*) too is the house of *Venus*. — These *domini* (Arab. *ṣāḥib* or *rabb*) stand in Batt.'s rose in the second innermost circle.
@p 0135L31..0135L37

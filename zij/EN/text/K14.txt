This is done by the same formula as in Batt., namely

What right ascension corresponds to the arc 25° of Aries? For this case we must first look up in Table 21 the declination (obliquatio) *δ* of the sun for the 25th degree of Aries; it is 9° 50′ 21″; Kh. takes the obliquity of the ecliptic as 23° 51′; the computation by the formula sin *a* = tg *δ* · cot *ε* gives (computed with five-place logarithms) the value 23° 5′ 50″ for *a*; our Table 59 has, at 25° of longitude (in the section *Horoscopus arietis*), the number 113° 5′ 49″; since Kh., like Batt. in the corresponding tables[^72.2], counts the right ascension from 0° of Capricorn instead of from 0° of Aries, one must subtract 3 signs = 90°, and so obtains *a* = 23° 5′ 49″.[^72.3] — Batt.'s values naturally differ from Kh.'s, since he takes the obliquity of the ecliptic = 23° 35′; Batt. also contents himself with degrees and minutes: in our example he has a right ascension of 113° 8′ (= 23° 8′).

That some Arab astronomers count the right ascension from 0° of Capricorn has its reason in this, that this right ascension, increased by 90°, then gives precisely the degree of the equator that rises at the same time as the degree of the ecliptic in question culminates; and this was important to know for astrological purposes. In our example, then, the point of the equator with the right ascension 113° 5′ 49″ rises at the same time as the point of the ecliptic with the longitude 25° culminates.[^73.1]

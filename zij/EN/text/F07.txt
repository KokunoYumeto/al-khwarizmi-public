In the text and in the tables there occur some quite corrupt *Arabic* words and sentences, which I should like to try to explain here, since I could not well place them in the glossary under a definite headword. How they came into the work is hard to decide, except for the Persian names of the planets; they seem to me mere games of the translator or of a copyist; it can hardly be supposed that they come from Maslama al-Majrīṭī, let alone from al-Khwārizmī. For the kind help that Professors Nallino in Palermo and Seybold in Tübingen gave me with the difficult passages, I express my sincere thanks to them here as well.

In Ms. O, on fol. 103$^{v}$ (Table 7), there stands the following note in the upper margin: *kulelach. vveli. kemithl. elhileli. fikemelen. wanuczeni.* This is probably meant to be *kull al-aḥwāli kamithl al-hilāli fī kamālin wa-nuqṣānin*, which means, freely translated: all changes (of life, of nature) behave like the moon; (they show themselves) in a waxing (up to fullness) and in a (following) waning.
@p 0025L19..0025L24
In the same manuscript, on fol. 104$^{v}$ (Table 9), above the column title *Heza Saturni supradictum*, there stands the following note: *Nota quod hoc argumentum felek eltedewir servit. bifelek eltedewir lieztekim asmir.* (note that this argument serves the *felek eltedewir* ...). — *Felek eltedewir* = *falak al-tadwīr* is the *epicycle* of the planets; *lieztekim asmir* might, as Nallino conjectures, mean *li-yastaqīm al-sair*, or *al-masīr*, that is, that it may determine, order, regulate the course (of the planet); and indeed the epicycle was introduced for this purpose.
@p 0025L25..0025L41 + 0026L01..0026L08
In the same manuscript, on fol. 127$^{v}$ (Table 55), there is the following note in the upper margin: *Cullel. kavvekib. kumna. fiscerafehu. ille Otari. ahine unxi ademu.* Nallino conjectured that the whole might be a verse in honour or praise of some high personage (a prince), and should read *kullu l-kawākibi qumna fī sharafihi illā ʿUṭārida*, that is, all the planets rose in his honour except Mercury; but the three words *ahine unxi ademu* remained unclear to him. — On a visit to Prof. Seybold in Tübingen in the autumn of 1913 I laid this passage before him too; it at once reminded him of some passages of the Koran[^XXI.1] that tell of the angels, who all fell down before *Adam* except *Iblīs* (Satan); and he therefore put forward the conjecture, surely well founded, that the *a* of *ahine* is probably corrupted from a *d* and belongs at the end of *Otari*, and that the *x* in *unxi* is to be read *sh*, after the Spanish manner of transcription, so that the last three words are to be read *ḥīna unshiʾa ādamu*, and the whole sentence, in half classical, half vulgar pronunciation, *kull al-kawākib qumna fī sharafihi illā ʿUṭārid ḥīna unshiʾa ādamu*, that is, all the planets rose in his (Adam's) honour except Mercury, at the time when Adam was created. — That *Mercury* here takes the place of *Satan* (*Iblīs*) probably has its reason in the fact that in the Middle Ages the Greek *Hermes* was identified with the *Hermes Trismegistos* or *Thoth* of the Egyptians, and he was the patron god of the alchemists, astrologers and magicians. In the books of miracles and magic of the Christian Middle Ages *Ophiel* is named as one of the seven princes of the underworld; this word is very probably compounded of the Greek *ophis* = serpent and the Hebrew *el* = god, thus serpent-god = *Hermes*; joined with the *megistos* of the word *Trismegistos*, *Ophiel* became *Megistophiel*, and from this arose the corrupted, or deliberately distorted, *Mephistopheles*. This is probably the best etymology of the word[^XXII.1].

Again in Ms. O, in the upper margin of fol. 132$^{v}$ (in our text p. 20), there stand the four words *eltabeb, helhacib, elhaazem, el-munegim*; the first, the second and the fourth are probably to be read *al-ṭabīb, al-ḥāsib, al-munajjim*, and then mean: the physician, the reckoner, the astronomer; the third I cannot interpret. What the scribe means to say by these words is unclear to me; perhaps he means that the following chapter, *Quot gradibus in qualibet regione quodlibet oriatur signum* (with how many degrees each sign rises in any region), is important for these professions.

In Ms. C, on fol. 83$^{r}$ (p. 27 of our text), after the words *et accipies quod contra invenies* (and you will take what you find opposite), there stand the following: *wakhod mebibelche*; Nallino believes, and probably rightly, that the last word must be read *mekibelche*, and the sentence would then be *wa-khudh mā qibālahā*[^XXII.2], that is, and take what stands opposite it (that is, opposite the argument); thus simply the Arabic text of the preceding translation *et accipies quod contra invenies*.
@p 0026L22..0026L38
In the titles of Tables 27, 33, 39 and 45 both Mss. C and O give the *Persian* name of the planet after its *Arabic* one each time; only in Table 51 is the Persian name of Mercury missing. Those of Saturn (*Keiwen* = *Kaiwān*), Jupiter (*Hormi* = *Hormuz*) and Mars (*Baheram* = *Bahrām*) I recognized, but those of Venus[^XXII.3] are so corrupt that I could not succeed in interpreting them. On fol. 122$^{v}$ (Table 45) of Ms. O, after the Arabic name of Venus (*elzohara*), there stand the following words: *beied hakht eneid*; on fol. 65$^{r}$ of Ms. C, after the same Arabic word, there stands *bei ed halt eneid*. In the last word, *eneid*, I did suspect the Persian name of Venus known to me, *nāhīd*, but the *e* before *neid* troubled me, for I did not know that the Persian name is also written *anāhīd*; *beied hakht* or *bei ed halt*, however, was quite unintelligible to me. Here again Nallino's knowledge of these things put me on the right path: *beied hakht eneid* is corrupted from *bēdukht anāhīd*; these are the names of two ancient Iranian female deities, which were later transferred to the planet Venus. — It is very probable that these Persian names of the planets come from al-Khwārizmī, for Maslama will hardly have understood Persian.

On the principles that have guided me in reproducing the text and the tables I must remark the following.
@p 0027L03..0027L08
In the text the words are reproduced as they stand in the manuscripts, with these exceptions: first, for the genitive and dative ending of the first declension singular the modern *ae* has been written instead of the medieval *e*; secondly, for *u*, when it stands before a vowel and is therefore read *v*, this letter has always been put; thirdly, both manuscripts often (not always) write *c* for *t* before *i* and a following vowel; we have always written *t*.
@p 0027L09..0027L13
The text and the tables contain only Roman numerals, except for some later additions in O and N. In the text I have kept the Roman numerals, except in the addition by a later hand to chapter 21, which contains very many and rather large numbers and occurs only in C. In the tables, to make printing and reading easier, I have replaced all the numerals by Arabic ones.
@p 0027L14..0027L19
For zero three signs occur, in the text as well as in the tables: C and O have now τ (= *teca*)[^XXIII.1], now θ (= *theta*?), N always $\overline{0}$[^XXIII.2]. It is interesting that in N, in various places, some of which I have mentioned in the footnotes, XXX0 (or XX0) is written for 38 (a few times also for 28); so, in order not to let the number in Roman numerals become too long, VIII was replaced by 0, which is surely the initial letter of *octo*.

As for the tables, all the manuscripts except N show an incredible arbitrariness and inconsistency in the titles and in the headings of the columns: sometimes both the Arabic and the Latin title stand there, sometimes only the Arabic, sometimes only the Latin, sometimes no title at all. The headings of the columns, too, are often worded differently although they are meant to designate the same thing; thus, for example, in the same manuscript one table has *menses arabici* and another *menses arabum*, one *in annis collectis* and another only *in collectis*, one *via*, another *semita*, a third *catar* (= *linea*), one *recedentis* and another *descendentis*, etc.; often the column titles are left out altogether, especially in C. Sec.*, a fourth (especially in C, but not throughout) *P.*[^XXIII.5] *M.*[^XXIII.6], and these names are now abbreviated so, now written out in full.

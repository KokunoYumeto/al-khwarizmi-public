Batt. again uses the formula in the form $\sin DN = \dfrac{\sin\varphi\cdot\sin\delta}{\cos\varphi\cdot\cos\delta}$, whereas Kh., curiously, determines tg *φ* with the help of the gnomon, or takes it from the table of shadows (the table of cotangents)[^74.4]; but he does not multiply this tg *φ* by tg *δ*, but, in the various cases, by certain factors whose origin is not really clear and which also give only approximate results. Kh. first gives the rule for determining the *ascensio obliqua* for the whole sign of Aries; he says: multiply the noon shadow of the sun, when it is in the 1st degree of Aries (really the 0th), by 114 and divide the product by 115, and you have (in degrees) what must be subtracted from, or added to, the right ascension to obtain the *ascensio obliqua*.

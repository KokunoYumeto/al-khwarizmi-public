In the last paragraph of this chapter reference is made to a table that gives the times elapsed from important events, such as the Flood, or the accession (or the death) of eminent rulers, up to a certain point in time. In the manuscripts this table does not stand in the first place, but because it is the first to be mentioned in the text, we have put it as Table 1. This table is also mentioned in the addition enclosed in square brackets (pp. 3–4), which occurs only in Mss. CMN. That this addition did not stand in Kh.'s Arabic manuscript, and cannot have been added by Maslama either, is proved by certain sentences of it, such as: *nos vero latinorum studemus utilitati, ideo et in primis tabulam suppositam praenotavimus, per quam ab eo quidem anno quo hic liber in nostrum sermonem translatus est* . . . . So the title *de numerorum praescriptionibus* must be translated. The author explains here what is to be understood by *anni collecti*, *anni plani*, *gradus*, *dakaicae*, etc., and how one must proceed in using the tables (as an example he takes those for determining the mean places of the planets). I have the following remarks to add to this chapter. In *anni plani*, *planum* is the translation of the Arabic *mabsūṭ* = spread out; instead of *planum* one therefore also finds (in C) *expansum*; we should say *anni singuli*; N has *anni residui*. — The first column of the tables[^36.3] for determining the mean places of the planets usually contains the path of the planet from the *radix* in periods of 30 years; then follows the column for the single years from 1 to 30, then the column for the months, and so on. For the word “minute” C and O curiously often use the Arabic *daqīqa*, in its plural form *daqāʾiq*; this is rendered in Latin by *dakaica*, which is then declined like a genuine Latin word (for example *dakaicarum*). On the other hand, especially in Ms. C, the Arabic singular form *thenia* = *thāniya* is put for “second”, and from it the plural *theniae* is formed, instead of the Arabic plural form *thawānī* being used. For the rest, Ms. C mostly has *puncta* instead of *minutae* and *minuta* instead of *secundae*[^36.4]. — I have already drawn attention in the introduction to the arbitrariness and inconsistency that reign in these headings of the columns.

3) Table 3, which takes up one page in each of the manuscripts C and O, is spread over two pages in N (9$^{r}$ and 9$^{v}$): some columns are added here that are missing in C and O, but that occur in the *Tabulae spuriae* which Nallino added to his edition of al-Battānī (II, pp. 300–307). All the spurious tables of al-Battānī can therefore be traced back to Maslama's reworking of the tables of al-Khwārizmī, and the conjecture that Nallino expressed about these tables (II, pp. 308–309: *Haud improbabile est tabulas p. 302–307 ex opere Maslamah quoque, ut de p. 300–301 affirmatur, exscriptas esse, etc.*, it is not improbable that the tables of pp. 302–307 too were copied from the work of Maslama, as is asserted of pp. 300–301, etc.) has thus proved true. The columns added in Ms. N are the following:

A) On fol. 9$^{r}$, after the column *Anni Alexandri collecti*, which is the second column of our Table 3, there comes a column headed *Anni iazdeiardi* (*Yezdegirdi*) *post aeram arabum* (the years of Yazdegerd after the era of the Arabs); it agrees with column 4 of al-Battānī (II, 304), headed *Anni Persarum sine intercalatione, etc.* (the years of the Persians without intercalation), except for the radix, which is given there as 9$^{a}$ 11$^{m}$ 9$^{d}$ (see also Table 1, where the incorrect radix 9$^{a}$ 11$^{m}$ 4$^{d}$ is found too).

B) On fol. 9$^{v}$ there is the column corresponding to the preceding one for the *anni plani* or *residui* (the single, or remaining, years); it stands after the column *Anni Alexandri plani*, which is the fourth column of our Table 3. It agrees with column 4 of al-Battānī (II, 305), headed *Anni singuli Persarum sine intercalatione* (the single years of the Persians without intercalation), except for the days for *annus* 27, where our table has 19 but al-Battānī 18.

C) On fol. 9$^{r}$, immediately after column A for the *Anni iazdeiardi*, there is one for the *Anni Diocletiani completi* (the complete years of Diocletian); on the whole it agrees with col. 3 of al-Battānī (II, 304), headed *Anni Coptorum intercalationem habentes etc.* (the years of the Copts that have intercalation). With various Arab astronomers the *era of Diocletian* (beginning on 29 Aug. 284 AD) was also called the *era of the Copts*, a name by which various other eras were designated as well, such as the *era of Nabonassar*, that of *Philip Arrhidaeus*, and, in al-Battānī, also the *era of Augustus* or *Alexandrian era*, about whose beginning there is no full agreement[^XVI.1]. The column in N shows several deviations from that of al-Battānī: under a) it should read 483 instead of 488, under b) 977 instead of 997; and the last four lines should then of course read:

We give below the geometrical explanation of the above procedure for determining the true longitude of the sun according to Ptol.-Batt., namely according to the eccentric hypothesis, that is, that the sun moves in a circle eccentric to the ecliptic, not on an epicycle. Ptol., moreover, already showed that one arrives at exactly the same result with the help of an epicycle.
@p 0076L15..0076L32
Let *E* be the centre of the ecliptic, thus the centre of the earth, and *M* the centre of the eccentric circle in which the sun is supposed to move uniformly[^n]. *A* is then the apogee and *P* the perigee of the sun; let the sun be at the point *S*, and let *F* be the vernal point. Now ∢ *FMS* is the mean longitude of the sun; from it one subtracts the longitude of the apogee, that is, ∢ *FMA*, and then has ∢ *AMS*, the so-called *mean anomaly*, usually called *argument* in our tables. But we want ∢ *AES*, under which the arc *AS*, which the sun has run through from the apogee, appears as seen from the earth (*E*); we obtain this angle if we subtract from the mean anomaly ∢ *α*, the so-called *equation* of the sun. ∢ *AES*, or *AMG* if *MG* ∥ *ES*, is now called the *true anomaly*; if one again adds to it ∢ *AMF*, that is, the longitude of the apogee, one has ∢ *FMG* or the arc *FG*, that is, the *true longitude* of the sun. — In our text the equation *α* is subtracted directly from the mean longitude of the sun, which of course comes to the same thing.

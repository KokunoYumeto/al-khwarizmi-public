@obs L01-L05 end chapter 28a (p. 21, JP2 0053, L28-L30): the shadow s squared and 144 added; the side of the square, «elgidher» (al-jidhr, the root), is the hypotenuse, «diametros»; the shadow times sixty divided by it is a sine, whose arc subtracted from ninety is the altitude. Checked: s·60/√(s²+144) = 60 cos h for the shadow s = 12 cot h of a gnomon of twelve digits, whose arc is 90° − h; ninety less it is the altitude h.
@obs L06-L15: chapter 28b, the reversed shadow, «umbra elmankuz» (al-ẓill al-mankūs), from the altitude: the sine of the altitude («elgeib primum») times twelve, divided by the sine of its complement («elgeib secundum»), gives the reversed shadow in digits and minutes. Checked: 12 sin h / cos h = 12 tan h, the shadow of a gnomon of twelve digits set at right angles to a wall. In the left margin beside L07 «O 133r»; beside the heading of chapter 29 (L16) «C 76r, O 133v».
@obs L16-L24: chapter 29: «elbuht» (al-buht), which the text renders «stupor», is the arc that a planet travels in a given time, found as the difference of its places on two successive days, or for an hour or a moment. L25-L27: chapter 30, the size of the sun's disc, continued on p. 23. «stupor» is in italics in L18 and in roman type in L23 and L27.

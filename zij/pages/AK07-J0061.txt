@obs L01-L10: the parallax in latitude, continued from p. 28: a southern declination of the midheaven is added to the latitude of the place, giving the distance of the midheaven from the point above our heads («imminentis capitibus nostris», the zenith); the moon's latitude, northern, is subtracted from that distance and, southern, added to it, giving the moon's distance from the zenith, with which the table gives the parallax in latitude; it is northern when the midheaven is north of the zenith and southern when it is south of it. Checked: the parallax displaces the moon away from the zenith, towards the north when the moon stands north of the zenith and towards the south when it stands south of it.
@obs L11-L25: chapter 35, the solar eclipse: a conjunction by day with the sun near the head or the tail of the dragon; for the conjunction between the ascendant and the midheaven, east of the meridian, the parallax in longitude is subtracted from the place of the conjunction and the hours of the parallax from the hours of the conjunction; west of it both are added. Checked: east of the meridian the visible conjunction comes before the true one by about the time in which the moon gains the parallax on the sun, and at that time the moon's true place is less than the place of the conjunction by about the parallax; west of the meridian both are more. For the hours this is the opposite of chapter 34 (p. 28, JP2 0060, L20-L22).

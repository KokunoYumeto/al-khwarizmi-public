@obs L06-L35. The table gives the equation of time, «examinatio dierum», the difference between true and mean time (Suter, p. 80, JP2 0112, L11-L21), for every degree of the sun from Aries 1° to Virgo 30°, in minutes and seconds of time; Tab. 68 (p. 182, JP2 0214) continues it from Libra to Pisces. The seconds are mostly multiples of 4, whole minutes of arc of the equator at 4 seconds of time each; they are not at Taurus 28° and 29° and Gemini 3° and 4°, 23m 26s, 23m 27s, 23m 27s and 23m 26s, beside the second greatest value, 23m 28s at Taurus 30° to Gemini 2°, at Leo 8° and 9°, 14m 41s and 14m 42s, after the least value of this page, 14m 40s at Leo 1° to 7°, and at Gemini 9°, 11°, 27° and 29° and Leo 16°, 23m 6s, 22m 54s, 20m 14s, 19m 54s and 15m 10s, half minutes of arc.
@obs L06-L35. Checked, with Tab. 68: the 360 values are the sum of 4° 30′, of the reduction λ − α to the equator of the longitude 2° less than the argument, α its right ascension with the obliquity 23° 51′, and of 2° 23′ · sin(λ − 2s 24° 41′), converted to time at 4 minutes to the degree: within 1′ of arc (4 s of time) in 293 rows and within 2′ (8 s) in 357, the exceptions Aries 6° (L11), 9m 52s, 8.6 s less, and Sagittarius 26° and 27° on p. 182. With the reduction taken at the argument itself the departures reach 15′ of arc (60 s of time). The equation of time computed in the usual way from al-Khwārizmī's own values, the obliquity 23° 51′, the solar apogee 2s 17° 55′ and the greatest equation of the sun 2° 14′ (p. 9, JP2 0041; p. 45, JP2 0077, L07), with its least value set to zero, departs from the table by up to 2m 15s, at Sagittarius 26°.
@obs Notes on the readings.

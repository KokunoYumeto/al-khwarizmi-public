@obs L09-L38. Checked: the hourly motions are the mean hourly motions less a term in the cosine of the anomaly α: 2′ 27″ 50‴ − 5.6″ cos α for the sun, with the mean motion of p. 65 (JP2 0097, L20-L21), and 32′ 56″ 27‴ − 2′ 43.9″ cos α for the moon, with the mean motion as Suter corrects it there (L17-L19). The coefficients are the hourly motions in anomaly, 2′ 27″ 50‴ and 32′ 40″, times the rates at which the equations of Tables 21-26, 2° 14′ and 4° 56′ times δ : 23° 51′ (p. 132, JP2 0164), grow at 0°, 2° 14′ : 23° 51′ · sin 23° 51′ and 4° 56′ : 23° 51′ · sin 23° 51′. The second column agrees within 0.6″ and the third within 1.1″ in every row; the least motions, 2′ 22″ and 30′ 12″ (p. 78, L19-L21), are 2′ 22.2″ and 30′ 12.5″ by this law.
@obs L09-L38. Checked against the relations of Suter's commentary: the sixth column is five times the hourly motion of the sun and the seventh 1 3/5 times that of the moon (p. 90, JP2 0122, L11-L18), within 3″ and 1″ of the rounded motions of the second and third columns and within 1.0″ and 1.5″ of the law above; their difference, the radius of the earth's shadow (L16-L18 there), grows from 36′ 27″ at 1° to 37′ 0″ at 30°. The fourth column, the radius of the sun, is 6.6 times the hourly motion of the sun, from 11/20 of the daily motion for the diameter (p. 79, JP2 0111, L01-L03), within 1.3″ of the law; the fifth, the radius of the moon, is 0.4846 times the hourly motion of the moon within 0.7″, where 29/720 of the daily motion for the diameter (L03 there) gives 0.4833 times and values 1.6″ to 2.9″ smaller.

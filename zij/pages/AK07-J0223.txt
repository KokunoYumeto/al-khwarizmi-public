@obs L09-L38. The argument, 1° to 60° (61° to 150° on p. 192, JP2 0224), is for the parallax in longitude the hour angle of the moon and for the parallax in latitude its zenith distance at the culmination of its point of the ecliptic (Suter, p. 92, JP2 0124, L14-L29); the parallax in longitude is given in time, the hours by which the apparent conjunction differs from the true (p. 93, JP2 0125, L24-L28). Checked: the times agree within 6s with t = 1h 36m · sin(x + 15° · t): the time in which the moon, gaining 30′ 28″ an hour on the sun (32′ 56″ − 2′ 28″, Tab. 63, p. 177, JP2 0209), covers the parallax 48′ 45″ · sin(x + 15° · t) at the hour angle reached at the apparent conjunction, 15° an hour later for every hour of t (48′ 45″ : 30′ 28″ = 1h 36m). The exception is 24° (L32), 1h 0m 25s for 1h 0m 37s: see the critical note. The steps of the column fall from 2m 53s to 12s.
@obs L09-L38, continued. Checked: at every fifth degree the parallax in latitude is 48′ 45″ · sin x, the value at 90° on p. 192, within 0.5″; between these the values are interpolated linearly, the steps within each group of five degrees differing by 1″ at most (51″ from 0° to 5°, 27″ or 28″ from 55° to 60°), so that they lie up to 3″ below the sine. Suter's example, the zenith distance 56° 43′ 45″ (p. 94, JP2 0126, L04-L05), gives by interpolation between 56° and 57° (L34-L35) 40′ 43.4″, his 40′ 43″.
@obs Notes on the readings. Checked: the edition's readings follow the laws above: C's hour 1 at 24° and 30° (notes a-a, «a)—a)», against O's 0); the parallaxes in latitude 32′ 35″ and 41′ 18″ at 42° and 58° (notes e and f, against the 15 and 13 of C and O; 32′ 37″ and 41′ 20″ by 48′ 45″ · sin x), which give the steps 37″ and 38″, 27″ and 28″, where 15 and 13 would give 17″ and 58″, 22″ and 33″. The arguments 40, 50 and 52 (notes b, c and d) are C's, where O has 10, corrected from an uncertain reading («corr. ex?»), 49 and 50.

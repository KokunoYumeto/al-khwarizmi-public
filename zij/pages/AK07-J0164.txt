@obs L11-L40, the arguments: the first two columns count 0s 1° to 1s 0° (with the readings of notes a and b), the second two the complements 11s 29° to 11s 0°, for which the same values serve (Suter, p. 59, JP2 0091, on the heads «Computatio minuenda» and «Computatio augenda»).
@obs L11-L40, the declination of the sun («Obliquatio Solis»). Checked with the obliquity 23° 51′ (Suter, p. 58, JP2 0090): sin δ = sin 23° 51′ · sin m agrees to within 1.1″ in 28 rows (with the readings of notes g-l), the argument 1s 0°, 11° 39′ 50″, exactly; at 7° the table has 2° 49′ 22″ against 2° 49′ 28″ computed, at 13° 5° 13′ 50″ against 5° 13′ 7″, 43″ more, where the steps from 12° and to 14°, 24′ 30″ and 22′ 58″, show the excess. No note bears on these two values; Tab. 26 (p. 137, JP2 0169), which repeats these values in reverse order for 150°-180°, has the same two at 173° and 167°.
@obs L11-L40, the equations of the sun and the moon («Examinatio»). Checked: they are not proportional to the sine of the argument but to the declination δ of the same argument: the equation of the sun is 2° 14′ · δ/ε and that of the moon 4° 56′ · δ/ε, ε = 23° 51′, so that both reach their maxima, 2° 14′ and 4° 56′ (Suter, p. 45, JP2 0077, and p. 46, JP2 0078), at 90°. The thirty values of the sun agree with 2° 14′ · δ/ε to within 1.1″ (with the readings of notes c-e), those of the moon with 4° 56′ · δ/ε to within 1.1″ (with the reading of note f), except at 21° and 22°, 1° 43′ 22″ and 1° 48′ 2″, 2″ and 5″ less than computed. The law 2° 14′ · sin m would give 1° 7′ 0″ at 30°, where the table has 1° 5′ 32″ (computed 1° 5′ 32″).
@obs L11-L40, the latitude of the moon («Latitudo Lunae»). Checked: the values are 4° 30′ · sin m, the greatest latitude 4° 30′ at 90° (Suter, p. 59, JP2 0091) times the sine of the argument, to within 1.6″ in all thirty rows (with the readings of notes m and n), 2° 15′ 0″ at 30°; the rule sin β = sin 4° 30′ · sin m gives up to 6.6″ less.

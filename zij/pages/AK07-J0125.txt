@obs L08-L23. Checked: with W the autumnal point, the longitude 180° + WM = 303° 20′ and the right ascension 180° + WR = 305° 43′ give WM = 123° 20′ and WR = 125° 43′. The midheaven of the example, 228° 40′, with its right ascension 226° 10′, lies 48° 40′ and 46° 10′ beyond W in the order of the signs, so that for these numbers its longitude and right ascension are 180° + WE and 180° + WA, not 180° − EW and 180° − AW as in L20-L22, which follow the figure with E and A before W; the hour angle is in either case the arc AR of the equator between the meridian and the hour circle of the moon, here WR − WA = 79° 33′. The angle APR at the pole, s, measures the arc AR.

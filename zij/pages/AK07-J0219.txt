@obs L07-L29. The arguments of latitude, 10° 50′, 10° 30′ and then every half degree to 0° 0′, and their supplements, 169° 10′ to 180° 0′, with the reading of note a. The lunar eclipses at the greatest distance (Suter, pp. 86-88, JP2 0118-0120): the digits of the eclipse, the casus, from the first contact to the beginning of the totality or, in a partial eclipse, to the middle, and the half mora, half the totality, in minutes of the moon's path. Checked: the central row gives R + r = 29′ 16″ + 21′ 22″ = 50′ 38″ and R − r = 21′ 22″, the radius of the moon r = 14′ 38″, the value of Tab. 61 at the apogee (p. 175, JP2 0207), and the radius of the shadow R = 36′ 0″; the casus at 0° is the diameter of the moon, 29′ 16″. With the latitude β = 4° 30′ · sin x of Tab. 21 (p. 132, JP2 0164), 50′ 45″ at 10° 50′, larger than R + r, so that no eclipse occurs there, the casus with the half mora, √((R + r)² − β²), agrees within 20″ in the rows of total eclipse from 4° 0′ to 0°, and the half mora, √((R − r)² − β²), within 16″; at 4° 30′, the first row of total eclipse, the table has 41′ 4″ and 4′ 0″ for 43′ 12″ and 2′ 47″. The casus of the partial eclipses exceeds √((R + r)² − β²) by 2″ to 30″, and by 55″ at 9° 0′ (L11), 28′ 50″, where Tab. 74 (p. 188, JP2 0220) has O's 28′ 7″ and C the 50 of this page; at 8° 30′ and 5° 30′ (L12, L18) the printed 31′ 53″ and 43′ 56″ are 43″ and 25″ more than the computation, where Tab. 74 has 31′ 13″ and 43′ 36″: see the critical notes. At 1° 0′ the casus, 29′ 52″, 18″ more than the computation (29′ 34″), interrupts the decrease of the column, 30′ 3″, 29′ 52″, 29′ 19″, 29′ 16″.
@obs L07-L29, the digits. @obs L07-L36, the table of proportion: the arguments, the anomaly 2°-60° and 360° less it, 358°-300°, with the readings of notes d-g, and the sixtieths, «dakaicae superationum», by which the differences between the values at the far and at the near distance are multiplied (Suter, p. 88, JP2 0120, L22-L28). Checked: the values grow from 0′ 2″ at 2° to 14′ 0″ at 60°; they agree within 18″ with the share by which the distance of the moon on its epicycle of 5p 10′, √(60² + 5;10² + 2 · 60 · 5;10 · cos x), has fallen from its greatest value, 65p 10′, towards its least, 54p 50′, in sixtieths (14′ 4″ at 60°), being 8″ to 18″ larger from 14° to 30°; the fraction (1 − cos x) : 2 of the versed sine gives 15′ 0″ at 60°. The first values, 2″, 6″, 12″, 20″, 30″ and 42″ at 2°-12°, are n (n + 1) seconds for x = 2n.

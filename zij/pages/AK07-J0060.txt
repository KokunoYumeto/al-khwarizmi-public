@obs L01-L04: the sun or the moon is at the far distance in the last degree of Gemini or the first of Cancer, at the near distance in the last of Sagittarius or the first of Capricorn; N (note 1) has the eighteenth degree of Gemini and of Sagittarius. Checked: the apogee of the sun in chapter 8 is two signs 17° 55′ (p. 9, JP2 0041, L17), in the eighteenth degree of Gemini, where N puts the far distance, with the near distance opposite it in the eighteenth degree of Sagittarius; the last degree of Gemini of the text is the summer solstice. N's addition puts the moon's far distance at anomaly («heza») 0 and its near distance at six signs, half a revolution of the anomaly later.

@obs L07-L23: the parallax in longitude: the ascendant («horoscopus») at the hour of the conjunction less three signs gives the «medium caelum»; the right ascensions of that point and of the moon from the table of the right sphere («circulus aequinoctialis», «felek elmustakim»), and their difference, the moon's distance from it; with that distance the table of the parallax gives hours and parts, which multiplied by the moon's hourly motion give the parallax in longitude. Checked: the point of the ecliptic three signs before the ascendant is its highest point above the horizon, where the parallax in longitude vanishes; it is the culminating degree only when that degree is a solstitial point.
@obs L20-L22: «hoc productum» is added to the hours of the conjunction for the moon between the ascendant and the midheaven, east of the meridian, and subtracted for the moon in the west. Checked: in the east the parallax moves the moon towards the following signs, the direction of its motion, so that the moon is seen to reach the sun before the true conjunction and the hours of the visible conjunction are fewer than those of the true one; in the west they are more. The text has the opposite. N's marginal addition at note 19 says that the product is a quantity of degrees and minutes, not of time. To be compared with Suter's commentary on chapter 34.
@obs L24-L29: the parallax in latitude: the declination («obliquatio») of the midheaven, northern and less than the latitude of the place, is subtracted from the latitude; if greater, the latitude is subtracted from it (continued on p. 29). Checked: for a northern declination less than the northern latitude of the place, the distance of the point on the meridian from the zenith is the latitude less the declination, and for a greater declination the declination less the latitude.

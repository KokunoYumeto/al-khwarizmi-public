@obs Between L07 and L08 a figure in the full width of the page; it is not reproduced. It is the figure that stands at the end of chapter 23 in Ms. N only, as Suter says on p. 71 (JP2 0103, L10-L22), where he explains it. A circle divided into the twelve signs, numbered I to XII in italic Roman numerals in the arcs, I to VI from the top down on the left, VII to XII from the bottom up on the right; the vertical diameter, the horizontal diameter, and the chords that join the points of division: two horizontal chords above the diameter and two below it, and two vertical chords on either side of the vertical diameter, which together form a tall and a wide rectangle. Along the upper half of the vertical diameter, written upwards, «Sinus diminuti»; below the horizontal diameter, above the lower side of the wide rectangle, «Sinus plani». Above the horizontal diameter, which small strokes divide into six parts, six times «XX»; below it, in the segments between the circle and the vertical chords and between the chords, from the left: «Gr. VIII M. II S. XIX», «Gr. XXI M. LVII S. XLI», «Gr. XXX», «Gr. XXX», «Gr. XXI M. LVII S. XLI», «Gr. VIII M. II S. XIX», each abbreviation and number in a line of its own. All the letters of the figure are italic.
@obs The figure. Checked: on a radius of 60 the diameter has 120 parts, six times XX, and the strokes on it stand 20 parts apart, as measured on the scan (about 227 px for 20 parts on a diameter of 1361 px). The vertical chords join the points of division at 30° and 60° from the horizontal diameter, and they cut it 60 cos 30° = 51;57,41 and 60 cos 60° = 30 parts from the centre: the segments are 60 − 51;57,41 = 8;2,19 (the versed sine of 30°), 51;57,41 − 30 = 21;57,41 and 30, as labelled, and their sum is 60. The half chords of the rectangles are the sines of 30° and 60°, 30 and 51;57,41.

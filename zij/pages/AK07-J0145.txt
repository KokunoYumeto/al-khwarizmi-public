@obs L05-L28, the collected years. The years of Alexander are counted here in years of 365 1/4 days, «secundum bissestum», of twelve months of 30 days and 5 1/4 added days (the «Partes» are quarters of a day); Suter explains the reckoning with examples on pp. 38-39 (JP2 0070-0071). Checked: the radix, 932 years 9 months 17 days, is 287 days after the beginning of the year 933 of Alexander on 1 October 621, that is Thursday 15 July 622, the epoch of the tables (p. 39, JP2 0071). Each row adds 30 Arab years, 10 631 days, that is 29 years 1 month 8 3/4 days: the rows from the radix to 600 agree to the quarter day (with the readings of notes b and c); 30 years carry over into the next year as 12 months and 5 1/4 days, as at 90 and 360.
@obs L26-L28, the rows 630, 660 and 690, given by C alone (note a). Checked: they are each 4 1/4 days too large; 10 631 days added to the row 600, 1514 years 11 months 1 1/2 days, give 1544 years 0 months 5 days, not 9 1/4, and the rows 660 and 690 continue from 9 1/4 by the same step (computed 1573 y 1 m 13 3/4 d and 1602 y 2 m 22 1/2 d). The edition prints the values of C; this is no misprint of the edition, and no critical note is made.
@obs L05-L34, the single years. Checked: the row p gives the days of the first p Arab years, 354 days and 355 in the leap years 2, 5, 7, 10, 13, 16, 18, 21, 24, 26 and 29, in years of 365 1/4 days, months of 30 days and days: all 30 rows as printed (with the reading of note d), the last, 29 years 1 month 8 3/4 days, the 10 631 days of a cycle. L06-L28, «Menses omnes». Checked: the months of 30 and 29 days in turn give 30, 59, 89, … 354 days, 1 month 0 days to 11 months 24 days, all twelve as printed. The last column holds the multiples of 28 from 1400 to 2016, as in Tab. 1 (p. 109, JP2 0141).

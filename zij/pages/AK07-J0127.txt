@obs L01-L06. Checked against al-Battānī's table of solar eclipses (Nallino II, 91): at the greatest distance of the moon the apparent latitude is 12′ 3″ for 7 digits and 9′ 20″ for 8, the minutes of incidence 28′ 34″ and 29′ 33″; at the least distance 12′ 20″ for 8 digits and 9′ 38″ for 9, the incidence 31′ 40″ and 32′ 37″; the latitudes advance by 2′ 42″ 30‴ a digit. For 10′ 10″ linear interpolation gives 7 + 113 : 162.5 = 7.695 digits, «7 dig. 42′», and 28′ 34″ + 0.695 · 59″ = 29′ 15″; 8 + 130 : 162.5 = 8.8 digits, «8 dig. 48′», and 31′ 40″ + 0.8 · 57″ = 32′ 26″. 8 dig. 48′ − 7 dig. 42′ = 1 dig. 6′; 32′ 26″ − 29′ 15″ = 3′ 11″.
@obs L07-L13. Checked: the table of correction (Nallino II, 89) has 44′ 0″ at 120° and 46′ 45″ at 126°, so that the anomaly 125° 7′ gives 46′ 21″ by linear interpolation, the «46 1/3» of L08; «genauer 46′ 17″» answers to an anomaly of about 125° 0′. 66′ · 46′ 17″ : 60 = 50.9′, «51′»; 191″ · 46′ 17″ : 60 = 147″ = 2′ 27″; 7 dig. 42′ + 51′ = 8 dig. 33′; 29′ 15″ + 2′ 27″ = 31′ 42″.
@obs L14-L16. Checked: 31′ 42″ : 34′ 34″ = 0.917 hours, 55.0 minutes, «c. 55m». The hourly motion 34′ 34″ is the moon's own (al-Khwārizmī's Table 63, p. 93, JP2 0125, L27). Al-Battānī's rule (Nallino I, 111) divides the minutes of incidence «per praecessionem Lunae [respectu Solis]», by the moon's motion in excess of the sun's; with the sun's hourly motion of 2′ 31″ (Nallino II, 88) that divisor is 32′ 3″, the quotient 59 minutes, and the beginning and end 4h 12m and 6h 10m.
@obs L17-L25. Checked: the true conjunction 18h 44m 29s (p. 83, JP2 0115); 18h 44m 29s − 1h 33m 30s = 17h 10m 59s, «rund 17h 11m», counted from the noon of 22 January, 5h 11m in the morning of 23 January; 5h 11m − 55m = 4h 16m; 5h 11m + 55m = 6h 6m; 8 dig. 33′ : 12 dig. = 0.71, «etwas mehr als 2/3». The subtraction agrees with the apparent place of the moon, 54′ ahead of its true place (p. 93, JP2 0125, L31-L33): in appearance the moon met the sun 1h 33½m, the time of 54′ of its motion, before the true conjunction.
@obs L26-L33 and N01-N03. Checked: Meeus's algorithm (Astronomical Algorithms, ch. 54) gives a central eclipse of the sun (γ = 0.972) at the new moon of 23 January 901, its greatest phase at 7.54h Terrestrial Time, 6.93h universal time with ΔT about 0.61h, 6h 37m Cordova mean time. With the sun at 303° 20′ (declination 19° 31′ south) and the latitude of Cordova 37° 53′, the sun rose there at about 6h 59m apparent time, «erst um 7h». The local times from Oppolzer's canon for Cordova are not recomputed here.

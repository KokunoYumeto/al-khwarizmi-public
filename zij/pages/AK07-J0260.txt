@obs Suter's third example (p. 101, JP2 0133, L21-L27) uses the first field: the ascendant at 5° of Pisces, the planet at 25° of Cancer. The column of Cancer has 117° 13′ at 25° (L12), and with the sextile 48° 19′ (L03) the sum is 165° 32′, more than the last number of the field, 144° 58′ (L13); the remainder, 20° 34′, falls between 18° 30′ at the end of Virgo and 21° 15′ at 5° of Libra (L13, L08), and the interpolation gives 3° 45.5′ of Libra for the left sextile, Suter's 3° 45′. Suter's numbers 117° 13′, 165° 32′, 144° 58′ and 20° 34′ are those of the table. That a sum larger than the last number of a field is diminished by it, and its place sought again in the same field, agrees with the rule of the heads: the last number of a field answers to a semicircle, 180°, of which the sextile, the quadrature and the trine, 60°, 90° and 120°, are a third, a half and two thirds; the columns then serve for the opposite signs named under the first (Virgo under Pisces, Libra under Aries).

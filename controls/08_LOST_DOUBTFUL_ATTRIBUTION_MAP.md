## Additional historically attested boundary

- **Book on History:** historically attributed and quoted by later authors, but lost. It is not manufactured from quotations.
- **Sundial and two astrolabe works in Ibn al-Nadim:** the sundial appears to survive; the astrolabe-use work is fragmentary; the construction work seems lost. Later codex adjacency does not make every neighboring tract authorial.

## Zij recension firewall

- The original Arabic *Zij al-Sindhind* is lost.
- Maslama al-Majriti edited the tables; Adelard of Bath likely translated that recension into Latin. Suter 1914 edits this derivative line.
- Ibn al-Muthanna's Arabic commentary is lost; Hebrew and Latin versions survive. It is commentary by another author.
- Other Latin manuscripts combine material from al-Khwarizmi, Yahya ibn Abi Mansur, al-Battani, Ibn al-Muthanna, Majriti, and later compilers.
- The Toledan Tables preserve some Khwarizmi-derived tables absent from Majriti, but their Arabic antecedents are lost.

No session may merge these witnesses into a synthetic Arabic “original.” Variant comparison belongs in a witness ledger and critical apparatus.

## Practical-astronomy firewall

Charette and Schmidl's item-level analysis controls over the older tendency to call the Ayasofya/Berlin aggregate one 59-chapter work. Explicit attributions, tentative arguments, anonymous texts, and likely non-authorial texts remain separate. The exact missing source payload is:

- Berlin Landberg 56 fols. 77v-97v.
- Ayasofya 4830 relevant fols. 183-200 and 231v-235r.
- The public PAL record's exposed images cover unrelated fols. 53r-80v and are not a substitute.

## Calendar firewall

De Blois identifies a single Patna/Khuda Bakhsh 2468 copy, made in Mosul in 632 H / 1234 CE. The Hyderabad edition perpetuates scribal errors and adds many new errors, especially in old Indian and abjad numerals. Layer 1 must preserve the printed witness exactly; suggested repairs belong only in Layer 3 until checked against manuscript authority.

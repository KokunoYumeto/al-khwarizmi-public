# al-Khwārizmī, *Kitāb al-jabr wa-l-muqābala* — the 1939 Cairo edition, line by line

Muḥammad ibn Mūsā al-Khwārizmī's book of algebra, as edited by ʿAlī Muṣṭafā Mušarrafa and Muḥammad Mursī Aḥmad (Cairo: the Egyptian University, Faculty of Science, publications no. 2, 1939), transcribed from the copy of Columbia University Libraries (QA32 .K45), photographed by Arabic Collections Online in 130 frames at 400 ppi. Every printed line of al-Khwārizmī's text is one line here, with its anchor `AK01-F###-L##` (frame, line).

**Done:** all 130 frames: the cover, the title page, the three facsimiles of the manuscript, the editors' preface and study (recorded, not reproduced), and al-Khwārizmī's text from the basmala through the geometric demonstrations of the three compound cases, the chapters on multiplication, on addition and subtraction and on division of roots, the demonstrations of sums and differences of roots, the chapter of the six problems, the chapter of the various problems, the chapter on transactions, and the chapter on mensuration to its end (the demonstration for the right triangle, the quadrilaterals, the three kinds of triangles, the circle, solids and the square inscribed in a triangle), and the book of wills through its third chapter, the bequests of the share of an heir, the chapter on bequests with a dirham, the chapter of completion (باب التكملة), and into the computation of the cycle (حساب الدور): marriage in illness, manumission in illness (باب العتق فى المرض), the bride-price in the cycle (باب العقر فى الدور), and the advance purchase in illness (باب السلم فى المرض) to the end of the text, with the copyist's colophon, printed pp. 15–106: 1604 lines and eighteen figures; and the back matter (frames 123–130): blank leaves, the back of the printed wrapper with the faculty's list of publications (described), and the endpaper, the due-date slip and the back board of the Columbia binding (recorded).

## What is reproduced

- **al-Khwārizmī's text**, as the edition prints it, letter for letter: the dotless final yā' where the print has it, the hamza and madda where they are printed and not where they are not, the tanwīn on the final alif where it is printed, the full stops and commas with the spaces the print sets around them. Where the print sets no word space after a letter that does not join the next, none is written (مماكانوا, العشرةوتثلث); after a joining letter, whose final form ends the word, the space is written. The print's spaces vary in width from line to line, and the distance between two letters inside a word depends on the letters, so each doubtful place is measured on the scan and compared with the same letters elsewhere. The gap is the nearest approach of the ink of the two letter bodies above the baseline, so that the tails of ر, ز and و, which reach under the next letter, do not count (`tools/census.py`, which measures and marks every gap of a line; `tools/clipgap.py`, `tools/bodygap.py`). Some values, in px at 400 ppi: after ة and the hamza on the line, which end a word, 1 to 14 where no space is set and 16 or more where one is (the boundary is narrow, and each case near it is noted in the page record); after و, 20 to 30 inside a word (وهو, وجذرا) and 40 or more before a space; after ا and د, 6 to 21 inside a word, depending on the next letter (الزيادة), so these are compared with the same pair; after the ligature لا, 20 to 26 before a letter inside a word (الاعداد, الأبواب, لأنه) and 29 or more before a space. After ر the gap above the baseline can be the same inside a word and before a narrow space (26 to 32 px on frame 50); there the tail decides: inside a word it reaches under the next letter, 8 to 19 px from it, and where it ends farther from the next word the space is written. A missing space is recorded where the gap is no wider than the same letters inside a word or set without a space elsewhere, and clearly narrower than where a space is set; in doubt, the space is written. The conjunction و is always transcribed joined to its word; its distance from the next letter depends on that letter (23 px before ع, up to about 40 before ك), and a gap after a word that ends in و is compared with the conjunction before the same letter. After a figure label whose last letter joins (ٮ, ح, the knotted ه), the space is written, as after any joining letter. The measurements behind each such reading are given in the page records. Where the print sets a space inside a word and none at its end, the line keeps the print's division and a critical note records it (frame 37, L20). Wider gaps with which the print spreads the letters of a word to fill a line, like the elongation strokes, are not transcribed (frame 42, L01). The print sets the ك of أكثر 15 to 23 px from the alif before it (and from the ligature لا in الاكثر), about the width of a narrow word space, every time the word occurs; this is how the edition sets the one word, and it is transcribed as one word, with the measured gap in the page records (frames 71 and 72). The word الله is printed as the usual ligature, which the font draws from the plain letters. Lines printed with a paragraph indent are indented here. The elongation strokes (kashīda) with which the print stretches some words to fill a line are not transcribed.
- **The letters that label a figure**: the print sets them with a short wavy line above; they are written `{ov:اب}` in the page files and set with a line above in the PDF.
- **Bold type and turned letters**: the edition prints the statements of some problems in bold (`{b:…}`, set in bold); a letter printed upside down is written `{inv:ج}` and set upside down, also inside a label (`{ov:ه{inv:ن}}`). The ن of the labels is printed upside down every time so far (frames 38 and 42).
- **The ب of the labels** is printed without its dot every time; it is written ٮ, the dotless form (for comparisons the tools read it as ب).
- **The ending ـقى** is printed in two forms: with the flat, sweeping tail of the final ى, or with a deep, round bowl hanging from the loop of the ق, which has the shape of the final ق (الق, فالق). The print uses the round form also where the yā' is certain, as in بقى and مابقى (frame 104, L16, L21) and تلقى (frame 105, L14), often for a run of lines (frame 104: the flat form in L02–L10, the round form in L16–L22). Both forms are transcribed ـقى; ق alone is written where the word ends in ق (الق, فالق, الحق, فوق).
- **The editors' note marks** in the lines, where they stand.
- **The figures of the text** (from frame 36), as images where they begin on the page: an untouched crop and a grey half-size copy, like the facsimiles. Their labels are described in the page records.
- **The cover and the title page**, line by line.
- **The three facsimiles of the manuscript** (frames 9, 28 and 39), as images: an untouched crop of the frame and a grey half-size copy (`figures/`, `figure_provenance.tsv`).

## What is not reproduced

- **The editors' preface, their study and their footnotes** (frames 11–26 and the foot of the text pages). They are the editors' own writing; Muḥammad Mursī Aḥmad died in 1989, so their text is protected in the EU until 2059. Each page records where they stand and how many printed lines they take (`editors_notes_withheld.tsv`). Readings of the manuscript that the footnotes report are recorded in our own words; a marginal gloss of the manuscript that a footnote reproduces is quoted, since it is the manuscript's own text (frames 43 and 44; `witness_variants.tsv`). Where a footnote's mathematics is wrong, the page record shows it: on frame 65 the editors' note (١) gives the roots of (5/12·x − 4)² = x + 12 as 24 and 25/24; the second is 24/25. On frame 71 the editors' note (٤) gives 22/7 as 3.1419; it is 3.1429 to five figures. On frame 88 the editors' note (١) divides the whole of the bequests, 4374/9360 of the estate, between the two legatees as five to eight, which is the division of the third alone (by the note's own amounts they receive 1390 and 2984), and writes 13 × 9360 as 964080 for 121680. On frame 96 the editors' note (١) gives the two bequests of the problem of frame 95 as 82 and 108 parts of 755; the second is 99, as the text has it, and with 108 the heirs' 565 parts are not seven shares; their note (٢) prints the constant 9/50 of its first line as 9/59.
- **The back of the printed wrapper** (frame 126): the Faculty of Science's list of its publications, in English, with a notice in Arabic of a book by Mušarrafa on descriptive geometry and the printer's imprint. It is described in the page record, not reproduced; it is not part of al-Khwārizmī's text.
- **Copy matter** of the Columbia copy: the binding, the bookplate, the library stamp and call number, the due-date slip, the pocket and labels of the back board, and a reader's pencil marks (underlines, rings, English glosses). They are recorded in the page records and `copy_provenance.tsv`.

The edition corrects what its editors judged obvious copying errors of the manuscript without marking them (preface, frame 12). This transcription follows the edition as printed; it is not a transcription of the manuscript. Where the print itself is in error, the line keeps it, and a critical note, kept apart from the transcription (`critical_findings.tsv` and the last page of the PDF), proposes the correction with its evidence. So far there are thirty-eight: the print names with ع a rectangle that the figure labels ج (frame 36, L13), and, thirteen times, the point at which the line حه is halved, for which the figure has no ع and the manuscript, in the facsimile of frame 39, writes ج (frames 38 and 41); a ج and a ن printed upside down (frame 37, L08; frame 38, L02); a word space set inside a word (frame 37, L20); on frame 42, the same ع for the point that halves the side of the square, thirteen times, a label of one letter that has lost its ا (L09), and the ن of five labels printed upside down; and on frame 43, L01, the same ع once more; on frame 45, L04-L05, a product stated without the الا of its second factor, as the editors also note; on frame 47, L08, the doubled root of nine called «like the root of nine» without «twice», which the editors' note supplies; and on frame 48, the end of the line of twenty labelled ه where the next page and the figure have د (L20), and «جذر تسعة» for «جذرا تسعة» (L16), as the editors' note also says; on frame 49, L21, the letter ح given to a point of the line هد, which already names a point of the vertical line, where the figure of the demonstration (frame 50) has the letter that the earlier figures use for ج; on frame 50, L02, the same point named once more with the ع that stands for ج; on frame 55, L17, a line printed in a lighter, damaged type, «متلها» with two dots where the same computation has «مثلها»; on frame 57, L14, a comma set inside the clause «فى الذى يخرج من هذا»; on frame 63, L04, a comma set at the end of the line between the number «خمسين» and the noun it counts, «درهما»; on frame 66, L02, «خمسة وعشرين» for the twenty-five parts of a hundred and forty-four of a square that the multiplication requires, as the editors' note says, and in L12 a remainder of 132 and 440/625 for 132 and 444/625, whose root the text then takes correctly; on frame 70, L04, a comma set between the verb «فاضرب» and its object; and on frame 73, L16, and frame 74, L06 (twice), the same ع-shaped sort for the point of the figure of the right triangle that the figure marks with the letter of ج; on frame 86, L05, «وتركث» with three dots for وتركت; on frame 87, L12, «يصبح» with a ب too many for يصح, the shares come out whole; and on frame 89, L01, the number from which the shares of that example are said to come out whole, 219,320, which is not divisible by three or thirteen; the least such number is 4680; and on frame 90, L07, «تسع مال», one ninth, for the two ninths that remain of the third, as the next step, eight ninths, requires; and on frame 99, L10, «سبعة وخمسون», 57, for the fifty-nine parts that the first bequest takes from what remains of the third, with which the estate of 1344 is accounted for exactly; and on frame 101, L10, «ثم الق ما يبقى معك», where the amount cast away, as the next words state, is a third of what remains (ثلث ما يبقى); and on frame 102, L13, «فاقسم سبعة», where the number to be divided by five shares and a half is seventeen (سبعة عشر), as the equation before it and the share after it, three and an eleventh, require; and on frame 103, L10–L11, where the words that give the completed estate in shares, five shares and two seventeenths, are missing between two occurrences of «سبعة عشر جزءا», and the line keeps only the dirham part of the result; on frame 107, L10, «ثاث», an alif set for the ل of ثلث; and on frame 107, L20, where a bequest is stated as less a seventh of what remains of the third, «الاسبع ما يبقى», while the working, and the result with it, takes two sevenths (frame 107, L22; frame 108, L01–L03); and on frame 110, L01–L02, where a hundred and ten less one thing remain, though the husband's legatee of a third also receives a thing, and the next sum and the equation deduct two («غير شيئين»); and on frame 113, L04, «سبعمائة درهم ونصف شىء», seven hundred and half a thing, where the sum is seven hundred less a thing and a half, as the restoration that follows requires; and on frame 116, L06–L07, a hundred and sixty-two and two twenty-sevenths of a dirham for the hundred and sixty-two and twenty-six twenty-sevenths that return to the master (the words for eight ninths are missing), with which the next total, five hundred and twenty-nine and seventeen twenty-sevenths, is computed; and in L09 «وثلث تسع شىء», thirteen twenty-sevenths of a thing, for the fourteen («وثلثى تسع شىء») that the next step halves to seven; and on frame 121, L11, «ستين», sixty, for «شيئين», the two things that thirty less a thing equals, as the restoration that follows requires. According to the editors' study (frame 26), the manuscript is kept in the Bodleian Library at Oxford. It was written in Cairo and finished on Sunday 19 Muḥarram 743 (23 June 1342), and it is the only copy the editors knew.

## How it was checked

- Every line was read on the frame at full resolution (400 ppi). Each doubtful letter, sign or word space was read again on enlarged crops before it was entered.
- The transcription was then compared word by word with an independent machine reading of the same lines (Surya OCR, run locally on each frame with its figures blanked; `tools/suryaprep.py`, `tools/suryacheck.py`). On the text frames 29–43, of 2,534 words, 188 differ: the machine's misreadings and spacing, and above all the labels of the figures and the bold type, which it reads poorly. Each was settled on the scan, or, on frames 29–35, which the machine read twice from different crops, by its other reading. One was an error of the transcription: توفيقى for the printed توفيق (frame 30, L06), now corrected.
- The word spaces after non-joining letters were measured as described above. Measured again this way, the joins recorded on frames 29–42 hold but ten. واحدونصف (frame 35, L08): the د and the و stand 38 px apart, as far as the word spaces of the line; now واحد ونصف. In the bold type of frame 37, L20, وأحدوعشرون: 12 px, which does not decide; now written with the space. فانانجعل (frame 37, L20): the print sets its space inside فانا, 29 px after the first alif and 15 px after the word; now فا نانجعل, with a critical note. لازيادة (frame 35, L03): 24 px between the ا and the ز, which stand 11 to 14 px apart without a space; now لا زيادة. واحدوعشرون and الواحدوالعشرون (frame 38, L05, L13) and واحدونصف (frame 42, L06, L14, L19) with ادوهو (frame 42, L18): 16 to 25 px between the د and the و, which stand 6 to 7 px apart inside a word; these were first taken for joins by comparison with the wider spaces of other lines, and are now written with the space.
- The round form of the ending ـقى was first read as a final ق on frames 061 (L01) and 104 (L16, L18), فيبق. The same form stands where the yā' is certain (بقى and مابقى, frame 104, L16 and L21; تلقى, frame 105, L14), so it does not show that the yā' is missing; the three words read فيبقى.
- The distances given for the full stops and the note marks only show whether a mark stands close to a word or between spaces. From frame 87 they are measured between the nearest ink pixels, centre to centre, which is one more than the count of blank pixels by which the word gaps are given; two stops are given as blank pixels (frame 89, L13, and frame 93, L10). The earlier frames measured them in their own way, and their values can be 1 to 3 px smaller. These differences are far smaller than the difference between a stop set close after a word (about 10 px) and one set between spaces (20 px or more). Checked against the scans again on frames 87–106, three pairs of values had been written in the reverse order (frame 96, L01, twice; frame 101, L03) and are corrected.
- `tools/check_obs.py` checks every Arabic word that an observation quotes with a line number against that line.
- The PDF builds deterministically: `python make_pdf.py --check` rebuilds it and compares the bytes.
